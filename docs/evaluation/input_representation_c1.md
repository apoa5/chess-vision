# C.1 — Input representation audit and architecture feasibility

Completed 2026-10-26. Offline research only; no training, new classifier inference, production changes, or final-holdout access.

The current representation demonstrably truncates a distinguishing king feature in one reviewed development example. It also misclassifies other pieces whose heads remain visible. Thus truncation is a confirmed representation limitation, but it does not establish the dominant cause of recognition errors or prove that a larger model is necessary. Neighbor interference, learned target attribution, domain shift, occlusion, and residual geometry all remain relevant.

**Review packet:** [side-by-side HTML audit](input_representation_c1_examples/index.html), [estimated visible extents](input_representation_c1_examples/visible_extents.html), [machine-readable cases](input_representation_c1_examples/audit.json), [measurements](input_representation_c1_examples/analysis.json). Fourteen deliberately selected diagnostic cases are not an accuracy sample. Ten are from D.2's human-reviewed grids and owner-confirmed/corrected labels; four are D.3c examples with provisional fine geometry and no reference-photo verification. Ground truth selected illustrative cases only; it is not an extractor input.

## Existing pipeline and information retained

Inspected `src/vision/{perspective,grid_geometry,crop_experiments,dataset_loader,classifier,training}.py`, the frozen D.2 evidence runner, D.3c native-video adapter, dataset grouping, and B.1–B.4/D.1–D.3c reports. The research and roadmap are at repository root (`VISION_RESEARCH.md`, `IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md`), rather than under `docs/`.

The unchanged B.2 `context1_224` checkpoint is `outputs/evaluation/crop_b2_20261018_run1/training/context1_224/best.pt`, SHA-256 `dad8eea28482ee894dfca0794d9f4415d21ac58db23c6d6bed8c4307c71b1bd8`. Its 6,247,145-byte artifact contains the four-channel MobileNetV3 Small classifier (1,531,325 model parameters). Frozen inference uses `configs/experiments/evidence_d2.json`.

Raw camera pixels are warped using the detected/reviewed board corners into an 800-pixel board with 200 pixels of surrounding context. Board-plane resampling is cubic. The original roughly 500×500 neighborhood is subcropped to approximately 300×300 for tighter B.2: target square plus one square of context on each side. RGB is resized to 224×224 using area interpolation, converted to RGB, and ImageNet-normalized. A fourth channel is the projected **ground-square footprint**, rounded and filled, then resized by nearest neighbor. It is binary, not a piece silhouette. RGB outside the footprint is retained. Crop pixels remain in camera orientation; semantic square IDs assign canonical coordinates without rotating every piece upright. Training and inference share the packed-input implementation.

For these 14 cases the B.2 target mask has an area-equivalent width of 72.4–76.2 pixels (median 75.2). The wider five-square context gives 43.5–45.8 pixels (median 45.4). These are mask-area diagnostics, not measurements of piece texture or sensor resolution. Interpolation cannot restore absent source detail. A correct plane homography rectifies the board, but treats elevated piece pixels as though they lie on that plane, stretching/shearing their appearance. This is a modeling limitation, not a confirmed homography-direction or square-index bug.

## Implemented offline prototypes

`src/vision/input_representations.py` implements deterministic extraction; the audit never calls a model. `scripts/audit_input_representations.py` binds each case to source/geometry hashes, canonical square, original timestamp, old prediction distribution and packed-input hash. All **14 frozen RGB+mask inputs match their D.2/D.3c saved hashes exactly**.

| Representation | Construction | Benefit to inspect | Cost/risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frozen B.2 | Existing tighter 224 RGB+footprint mask | Exact unchanged control | Plane distortion, clipped crowns, neighbor attribution |
| Taller native | Target footprint plus projected two-square-height envelope; 0.35-square margin; original unwarped RGB, aspect-preserving 224 letterbox; aligned footprint channel | Preserves native tall-piece appearance and more head support | Uncalibrated height/direction assumptions; neighbors remain; target can shrink |
| Expanded context | Existing two-square padding / five-square context, 224 RGB+mask | Wider spatial evidence with explicit target | Already supported by existing crop machinery; not a new invention. Less target detail and more interfering pieces |
| Two-branch proposal | Taller/local plus expanded/context, each with footprint channel | Separate local appearance from wider spatial attribution | Inputs implemented, classifier not implemented; approximately two backbone passes if separate encoders, plus head/activation cost |

The taller envelope estimates a camera-facing board normal from a homography under square-pixel, centered-principal-point and undistorted-pinhole assumptions. It is a crop heuristic, **not a calibrated camera pose or inferred piece height**. Unstable/near-overhead cases explicitly fall back to image-up extension; camera roll can defeat that fallback. All 14 actual examples used the assumed-pinhole path; a synthetic calibrated scene checks its projected top, and a separate test exercises fallback. No new localization or orientation fitting is performed. Two-branch arrays share storage rather than duplicating pixels.

Across these examples native taller target width ranges 55.1–107.0 equivalent pixels (median 72.3); retaining a crown does not automatically increase target detail. Median local Jacobian anisotropy is 1.226 for board-warped B.2 and 1.001 for native letterboxing. This measures additional extraction deformation at the base, not native perspective distortion or piece-recognition accuracy.

Synthetic ±2-pixel translations of all corners/square polygons, with source RGB fixed, give median RGB absolute changes of 9.97/255 (B.2), 7.92 (taller), and 8.90 (expanded). Minimum footprint-mask IoU is 1.000, 0.903 and 1.000 respectively. Warped masks can remain identical while their RGB content moves. These 56 perturbations are a limited sensitivity probe, not a robustness benchmark or justification for changing thresholds.

Combined extraction of all prototypes took median 21.1 ms per diagnostic target (15.8–54.0 ms on this CPU run). The prototype repeats a board warp per call; production-style shared caching would be needed before interpreting per-board cost. No model latency was measured here. Historical B.2/D.3c CPU classifier timings are not timings for these new inputs. The published visual packet is approximately 3.67 MB: 42 bounded JPEG previews, JSON and HTML; no new full-frame dataset, video copies, or tensor archive.

## Published evidence and available implementations

**Wölflein & Arandjelović (2021):** RANSAC localization, binary occupancy then occupied-piece classification, including taller piece crops, provide precedent for separating occupancy and shape context. Their synthetic benchmark reports 0.23% per-square error and adaptation to an unseen set from two starting-position photographs. These do not establish performance on our phone sessions. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2104.14963v2). [ChessCog](https://github.com/georg-wolflein/chesscog) supplies code/model download tooling under MIT; its recognition interface requires the player perspective and calls distinct occupancy/piece crop workflows. Native unwarped ROIs in this task are our engineering prototype, not a claimed faithful ChessCog reproduction.

**Masouris & van Gemert:** whole-image classification and relative-object recognition avoid a sequence of independent square classifiers. ChessReD contains 10,800 real smartphone photographs; reported exact-board accuracy is 15.26%, not square accuracy. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.04086v3). Their [implementation](https://github.com/ThanosM97/end-to-end-chess-recognition) links pretrained weights and documents Python 3.8.16 plus an important preprocessing caveat: released weights used offline 1024×1024 resizing, potentially differing from runtime resizing. No weights were downloaded or benchmarked here.

**Seytre (2026):** ChessQueries uses a ViT encoder and 64 learned square queries attending to the full image, directly outputting 13 classes per square. Its paper reports 99.2% exact boards on ChessReD and a 19-ms RTX4090 inference figure. Neither transfers to our dataset, CPU or phone. Global attention avoids a predetermined crop cutoff, but does not guarantee correct orientation or generalization. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.30762v1). Actual released smaller artifacts and implementation are assessed below rather than assuming the paper's large encoder is required.

**Govind & Arandjelović (2026):** Faster R-CNN recognizes pieces directly in source images; a separate Mask R-CNN predicts eight labelled boundary keypoints. Homography maps representative piece points into squares, avoiding plane warping of whole piece appearances. Reported synthetic exact-board accuracy is 76.61%, with class weighting, deeper heads and count/occupancy constraints involved. Those results are not a controlled crop comparison. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.09191v1). No author-specific repository or released chess weights were located in the paper/public search; its Detectron2 link is framework code. Detector boxes/keypoints add annotation and deployment cost. No detector was implemented.

These publications support testing alternatives; they do not prove that isolated-piece pretraining, a bigger backbone or a two-branch model fixes our failures.

## ChessQueries release and feasibility audit

Public source inspected at revision `f5aaaf38212cc7839b03ccf383455053905b3f7e`; source URLs and SHA-256 snapshots are in [public-source provenance](input_representation_c1_public_sources_v1.json). The original-paper ViT-S safetensors manifest specifies **63,147,914 bytes**, SHA-256 `486d10b258ba57d43d8ca861d20547093ca6591c0ad53da53a492716ef1c7705`, immutable release revision `eadee700749fd2930a651982c9f8a753097f81dd`. Despite its `chessquerieslite-vits-644-...` filename, this artifact is the original-paper small model, distinct from further-trained LiteV4. [Released model card](https://huggingface.co/joelseytre/chessqueries).

A bounded HTTP range request returned **206**, the expected total file length, and a valid safetensors header: 258 tensors, **31,560,077 tensor elements**, resolution 644, FP16, ViT-S DINOv2 encoder, four decoder layers and eight heads. This confirms an accessible weight artifact, not merely a README promise. Only the header range was fetched; the full payload SHA and a local forward pass remain **unverified**. The loader constructs without encoder pretrained downloads and loads checkpoint weights strictly. [Checkpoint loader](https://github.com/JSeytre/chessqueries/blob/f5aaaf38212cc7839b03ccf383455053905b3f7e/chessqueries/models/checkpoint.py).

Input is one already display-oriented RGB image, NCHW `[1,3,644,644]`, square bilinear antialiased resize without aspect preservation, division by 255 and ImageNet normalization. EXIF orientation is ignored; use D.2 decoded PNGs or apply native rotation exactly once. Do not accidentally use the transform helper's other default resolution. CPU prediction casts weights to FP32. [Transforms](https://github.com/JSeytre/chessqueries/blob/f5aaaf38212cc7839b03ccf383455053905b3f7e/chessqueries/data/transforms.py), [predictor](https://github.com/JSeytre/chessqueries/blob/f5aaaf38212cc7839b03ccf383455053905b3f7e/chessqueries/models/predictor.py).

Outputs are `[1,64,13]`: squares `a8…h8`, then rank 7 through `a1…h1`; class symbols `.PNBRQKpnbrqk`, matching our numeric class order after empty-symbol conversion. Queries encode square/file/rank identity; no ground-truth-assisted output rotation is allowed. Importantly, CLI full-FEN serialization supplies default `w - - 0 1` game-state fields; they are not visually observed state. Compare piece placement only and preserve explicit MVP game-state entry. [Board ordering/serialization](https://github.com/JSeytre/chessqueries/blob/f5aaaf38212cc7839b03ccf383455053905b3f7e/chessqueries/core/board.py), [model](https://github.com/JSeytre/chessqueries/blob/f5aaaf38212cc7839b03ccf383455053905b3f7e/chessqueries/models/chessqueries_model.py).

The inspected project targets Python 3.12, PyTorch 2.8, torchvision 0.23, timm, safetensors and NumPy 1.26, among other training/development packages. Local timm/safetensors/einops and related dependencies are absent, and local NumPy/torchvision differ from declared ranges. Use an isolated benchmark environment; no dependencies were installed into Chess-Vision. Minimal released inference avoids the entire training stack, but its imports must still be resolved. **Weights are available; local runnability and speed have not been demonstrated.** [Dependency declaration](https://github.com/JSeytre/chessqueries/blob/f5aaaf38212cc7839b03ccf383455053905b3f7e/pyproject.toml).

Code and weights use **PolyForm Noncommercial 1.0.0**: suitable adoption requires considering intended use; commercial MVP distribution is not automatically authorized. SLCC annotations and third-party broadcast images have separate terms. [License](https://github.com/JSeytre/chessqueries/blob/f5aaaf38212cc7839b03ccf383455053905b3f7e/LICENSE). No user images were sent to a public demonstration service.

ViT-S FP32 tensor storage alone is about 126 MB, versus B.2's roughly 6.25-MB artifact; activations and framework overhead are additional. A local/server-assisted research benchmark is feasible, with actual latency/RSS still to measure. The separate [LiteV4 release](https://huggingface.co/joelseytre/chessq-lite) includes iOS17+ CoreML artifacts around 62 MB and describes synthetic conversion smoke checks, not real-photo iPhone performance. Its proprietary additional training makes it unsuitable as a paper-faithful primary comparison. Browser/Android deployment and acceptable phone memory/latency are not established. The large paper model's GPU timing is not evidence for mobile viability.

## Controlled training design — not executed

Available approved historical data: **10,240 on-board crops from 160 boards/18 physical positions**, 7,168/1,600/1,472 train/validation/test rows across 12/3/3 position groups. Empty crops number 7,192; occupied crops 3,048. Occupied crops are not an isolated-piece scene dataset, and target masks are not segmentation labels. D.2 has 55 reviewed frames/five sessions, four positions overlapping historical training; D.3c has additional provisional geometry/labels and one known historical position overlap. Neither repeated frames nor changed FEN supplements create independent positions.

Use connected groups linking physical position, session and near-duplicate/source ancestry; c7 label correction retains the original physical identity. Never place related images across splits, even if a video or label version differs. The old inspected test set is development evidence. Use verified/frozen geometry for training comparisons, evaluation labels only for scoring, and no final holdout. [Design configuration](../../configs/experiments/input_representation_c1_design.json).

1. **Representation comparison:** unchanged B.2 control versus taller/native, wider context and local+context inputs. First compare single-branch inputs at 224, same MobileNetV3 initialization, optimizer, updates, training examples, validation selection and paired seeds 42/43/44 if affordable. Two-branch is a separately costed architecture arm, not an equal-compute representation-only result. Report context versus target scale jointly; a later fixed-target-scale arm would need a larger tensor and its own compute accounting. Do not feed new representations into the old checkpoint and interpret distribution-shift failure as the trained alternative's limit.
2. **Training curriculum, fixed representation:** compare standard complete-position/on-board crop training against isolated-piece pretraining followed by exactly the same on-board stage. Match initial pretraining update count with an on-board-only control; keep backbone/optimizer/real-data exposure and seeds paired. There is no registered verified isolated scene corpus. A small train-only synthetic isolated set or separately labelled isolated photographs would be needed for that arm. Rendering style generalization is a risk. Masking outside the square would truncate pieces and is not a valid isolated-piece substitute.
3. **Occupancy plus identity, fixed representation:** binary occupancy and conditional 12-class occupied recognition, with labels derived from existing verified FENs. Primary probabilities compose as `P(empty)=1-q`, `P(class k)=q*r[k]`; retain soft uncertainty rather than discarding all occupied evidence at a hard threshold. This is conditional factorization within one observation, not multiplication of correlated video evidence. Two independent networks can roughly double model storage and add occupied-crop passes; a shared backbone is a distinct architecture comparison. Any optional hard gate must later be chosen on training/validation groups only.

All arms should include naturally occurring train-only empty hard negatives next to tall pieces, border/spare-piece clutter, varied viewpoints and illumination, and balanced sampling/loss weights calculated on training groups. Preserve RGB/mask alignment. Track empty false positives, occupied misses, king/rook and black-rook recall, per-class confusion, exact boards, per-session results, latency, memory and parameter count—not only occupied accuracy. Match training budgets; additional pretraining data and dual encoders are explicit extra resources. Select using validation tradeoffs, report all attempted arms and paired session/seed results. Small physical-position counts do not support strong significance claims.

Existing verified FENs and grids suffice for the representation and occupancy arms; no new piece boxes are required to build the geometric prototypes. Precise silhouette completeness would require actual visible-piece segmentation/boxes and contact-point review; only that optional measurement or isolated-data arm warrants extra annotation. The h4 contact ambiguity should be reviewed before a causal training claim, without changing historical results. D.3c images do not become training-quality ground truth merely because their predictions are convenient. No new recording campaign is a prerequisite for the assisted MVP.

## Recommended next experiment

Run **one bounded, zero-shot original-paper ChessQueries ViT-S offline benchmark on all 55 reviewed D.2 frames**, using the separate corrected-label supplement, original square order, and the unchanged B.2 baseline on identical frames. First isolate dependencies, download/verify the entire declared weight hash, and execute a shape/order smoke test. Record source/transform hashes, per-session square/occupied/empty-FP/class metrics, exact boards, CPU latency and peak RSS. No ground-truth rotation, calibration fit, legal-position repair, training or threshold search. Five correlated sessions remain five development sessions; do not reuse the error-selected gallery as the benchmark. D.3c may be a separately qualified stress set, not a substitute for verification.

This cheaply tests a whole-image alternative before investing in new training. It cannot causally separate pretraining-data scale from architecture or representation, and its noncommercial license may preclude product adoption. A positive result would justify a subsequent explicit deployment/license decision, not automatic replacement. This recommendation is the **next task only**; no weights/model inference or MVP implementation was performed here. Assisted-MVP engineering remains a separate workstream.
